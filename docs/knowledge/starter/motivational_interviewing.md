# Motivational Interviewing (MI) — Coaching Application

How does motivational interviewing work? MI is a collaborative conversation style that strengthens a client's own motivation and commitment to change. The coach draws out change talk, responds to ambivalence with empathy, and uses structured skills (OARS) rather than persuasion or advice.

Motivational Interviewing is a collaborative, person-centred conversation style for strengthening a client's own motivation and commitment to change. It was developed clinically but is widely used in coaching to resolve ambivalence and build intrinsic drive.

## Core Spirit (PACE)

- **Partnership**: Coaching is done with the client, not to them. The coach is a companion, not an expert directing the client.
- **Acceptance**: Absolute worth of the person, accurate empathy, autonomy support, affirmation of strengths.
- **Compassion**: The coach actively promotes the client's wellbeing and prioritises their interests.
- **Evocation**: The client has the resources and motivation within them; the coach draws these out rather than installing them.

## Four Processes

1. **Engaging** — Building a working relationship and establishing trust.
2. **Focusing** — Collaboratively agreeing on the direction and purpose of the conversation.
3. **Evoking** — Eliciting change talk; exploring why and how the client might change.
4. **Planning** — Moving toward a concrete commitment and action plan once motivation is sufficient.

## Core Skills (OARS)

- **Open questions**: Invite elaboration. "What's most important to you about this change?"
- **Affirmations**: Recognise the client's strengths and efforts. "It sounds like you've been working really hard at this."
- **Reflective listening**: Reflect back the content and emotion. Simple reflection repeats; complex reflection adds meaning.
- **Summarising**: Periodically gather and link what has been said, highlighting change talk.

## Change Talk vs. Sustain Talk

Change talk is language that moves toward change (DARN-C):
- **Desire**: "I want to..."
- **Ability**: "I could..."
- **Reasons**: "It would help because..."
- **Need**: "I have to..."
- **Commitment**: "I will..."

Sustain talk is language that favours the status quo. The MI coach reinforces change talk and responds to sustain talk with empathy, not argument.

## Responding to Resistance (Discord)

- Roll with resistance rather than confronting it.
- Use double-sided reflections: "On one hand... and on the other hand..."
- Reframe: "It sounds like this matters enough to you that you feel stuck — that actually shows how much you care."
- Emphasise autonomy: "It's completely your choice what you decide to do."

## Coaching Questions for Ambivalence

- What are the good things about the current situation? What are the less good things?
- If you were to make this change, what would be different in your life?
- What concerns you most about staying where you are?
- On a scale of 1–10, how important is this change to you? What makes it a [X] and not a 1?
- What would it take to move one point higher on that scale?

## When to use MI in coaching

- Client appears stuck, demotivated, or conflicted.
- Client knows what they should do but isn't doing it.
- There is clear ambivalence between change and the status quo.
- A direct goal-setting approach (e.g. GROW) has stalled.
