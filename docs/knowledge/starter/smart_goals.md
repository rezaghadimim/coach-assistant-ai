# SMART Goals — Coaching Guide

SMART is a widely used framework for turning vague aspirations into clear, actionable goals. Coaches use it to help clients define what they want, how they will measure progress, and when they expect to achieve it.

## What SMART stands for

- **Specific**: The goal names exactly what will be done, for whom, and in what context. Vague goals like "be healthier" become "walk 30 minutes on three weekdays before work."
- **Measurable**: There is a clear way to track progress. Numbers, milestones, or observable outcomes make success visible.
- **Achievable**: The goal is realistic given the client's resources, constraints, and current capacity. Stretch goals are fine, but impossible targets erode motivation.
- **Relevant**: The goal connects to what the client values and to the broader coaching engagement. Ask: "Why does this matter to you?"
- **Time-bound**: There is a deadline or review date. Open-ended goals drift; time boundaries create urgency and accountability.

## Coaching questions for SMART goal-setting

- What exactly do you want to achieve?
- How will you know when you have succeeded?
- What resources do you have, and what might you need?
- Why is this goal important to you right now?
- By when do you want to achieve this?
- On a scale of 1–10, how confident are you that you can reach this goal?

## Common pitfalls

- Making goals too big — break large goals into smaller SMART sub-goals.
- Measuring activity instead of outcome — "attend five networking events" vs. "secure two informational interviews."
- Skipping the Relevant step — clients pursue goals that do not align with their values and later lose motivation.
- No review date — schedule a check-in to assess progress and adjust.

## When to use SMART in coaching

- At the start of an engagement to clarify focus areas.
- When a client has a vague aspiration and needs structure.
- During GROW model sessions at the Goal stage.
- When accountability check-ins need a concrete benchmark.
