# Coaching Homework and Exercises — Library

Between-session exercises reinforce insights, build new habits, and generate material for the next coaching conversation. Effective homework is specific, achievable, and chosen by the client.

## Principles for assigning homework
- Co-create with the client rather than prescribe.
- Start small — one clear action is better than five vague ones.
- Connect to what matters most to the client.
- Build in reflection — an exercise should generate insight, not just activity.
- Review at the start of the next session.

## Reflection and journaling exercises

### Daily check-in journal
"Each evening, write three things: one thing that went well today, one thing you want to do differently tomorrow, and one thing you are grateful for."

### Values reflection
"Write for 10–15 minutes on this question: What matters most to me, and how well is my current life reflecting those values?"

### Ideal day visualisation
"Describe in writing what your ideal working day looks like in detail — the environment, the activities, how you feel. What does this tell you about what you want?"

### Strengths inventory
"List 10 moments in the past year when you felt most alive, effective, or proud. What strengths were you using in each?"

### Decision-mapping
"For the decision you're facing, write two futures: one in which you choose option A and one in which you choose option B. Live in each for 10 minutes and notice what you feel."

## Behavioural experiments

### Small act of courage
"Identify one thing you have been avoiding because of fear or discomfort. Take one small step toward it this week and bring back what you notice."

### 'As if' experiment
"For one day, act as if you already had the confidence / clarity / energy you want. Notice what is different."

### Pattern interrupt
"When you notice yourself doing [the unhelpful behaviour], pause for 30 seconds before continuing. Write down what triggered it and what you chose to do."

### 30-day habit
"Commit to one small daily action for 30 days. Track it simply — a tick on a calendar is enough. Bring the results to our next session."

## Relationship and communication exercises

### Appreciation practice
"This week, give three genuine specific compliments to people in your life. Notice the effect on them and on you."

### Difficult conversation preparation
"Write out the conversation you need to have but have been avoiding: what you want to say, what the other person might say, and how you will handle it."

### Listening experiment
"In your next three significant conversations, focus entirely on understanding the other person before responding. Notice what you hear differently."

## Goal and progress tracking

### Weekly commitments tracker
"Each Monday, write 3 commitments for the week. Each Friday, review: what did you do, what got in the way, what will you carry forward?"

### Progress log
"Keep a running document of evidence of progress toward your goal — small wins count. Review before our sessions."

### Obstacle planning (if-then)
"For your top commitment, complete this: 'If [obstacle] happens, then I will [specific response].' Write three if-then plans."

## Mindset and wellbeing exercises

### Thought record (CBT-informed)
"When you notice a strong negative emotion, write: the situation, the automatic thought, the emotion and its intensity (0–10), evidence for and against the thought, a more balanced alternative thought, and how you feel after (0–10)."

### Energy audit
"For one week, rate your energy at the end of each activity (high / neutral / low). At the end of the week, identify patterns: what energises you and what drains you?"

### Worry time
"Set aside 15 minutes a day as 'worry time'. When worries arise outside that window, write them down and defer them. Notice whether worrying on schedule changes how much it intrudes at other times."

### Self-compassion letter
"Write a letter to yourself about a difficulty you are facing — the way a kind, wise friend would write to you. Notice what you would say and what that feels like."

## Questions to assign as reflection prompts
- "What is one thing you know you need to do but have been putting off? What is one step you can take this week?"
- "What would you do if you knew you could not fail?"
- "Who do you most admire and why? What quality of theirs do you want to develop?"
- "What story are you telling yourself about this situation? Is it helping you?"
- "What are you tolerating in your life that you should address?"
