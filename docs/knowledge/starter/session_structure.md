# Coaching Session Structure — Templates and Guidelines

Well-structured sessions create safety, focus, and accountability. This guide provides templates for different session types.

## Standard Coaching Session (45–60 min)

### Opening (5–10 min)
- Check in: "How are you coming into this session today?"
- Review since last session: progress on commitments, any changes in context.
- Confirm the focus: "What would you like to work on today, and what outcome would make this session valuable for you?"

### Exploration (25–35 min)
- Use GROW, MI, or CBT-informed questions based on the client's needs.
- Follow the client's energy and agenda, not a rigid script.
- Key activities:
  - Clarify the goal for the session
  - Explore the current reality
  - Generate options and insights
  - Test assumptions and beliefs

### Action planning (10–15 min)
- Identify 1–3 concrete next steps.
- Make commitments specific: who, what, when, how.
- Anticipate obstacles: "What might get in the way? How will you handle that?"
- Confirm support needed.

### Close (5 min)
- Summary of insights and commitments.
- Ask for session feedback: "What was most valuable today?"
- Schedule next session and set the focus.

## First / Intake Session (60–90 min)

### Welcome and contracting (10–15 min)
- Introduce the coaching relationship and process.
- Clarify roles: coach as thinking partner, client as expert on their own life.
- Discuss confidentiality, logistics, and expectations.

### Client background (15–20 min)
- "Tell me about yourself and your current situation."
- Professional role, key responsibilities, current context.
- What prompted seeking coaching now?

### Goals and aspirations (20–30 min)
- "What do you most want to achieve through this coaching engagement?"
- Short-term (this engagement) and longer-term goals.
- How will the client know if coaching has been successful?

### Initial assessment (10–15 min)
- Life/work balance wheel or similar tool.
- Current strengths and resources.
- Areas of challenge or development.

### Close and contracting (10 min)
- Summary of what was shared.
- Agreement on focus areas for the engagement.
- First session commitment.

## Progress Review Session

Use this structure every 4–6 sessions.

- What progress has been made since we started?
- What goals have been achieved, partially achieved, or changed?
- What patterns has the client noticed about themselves?
- What has been most valuable in the coaching?
- What adjustments, if any, to the focus or approach?
- What is the priority for the next phase?

## Session Agenda Setting Questions

- "What's most important to explore today?"
- "What has been on your mind since we last spoke?"
- "What would make today's session a 10 out of 10 for you?"
- "What do you most need support with right now?"

## Signs the session is working well
- The client is doing most of the talking.
- The client is discovering insights, not just receiving advice.
- The client seems energised or thoughtful, not passive.
- Commitments are specific and self-chosen.

## Signs to recalibrate
- The coach is talking more than the client.
- The client is agreeing with suggestions without conviction.
- The session feels like advice-giving or problem-solving for the client.
- Commitments have been the same across multiple sessions without progress.

## Documentation after each session
- Key topics discussed
- Client insights and breakthroughs
- Decisions made
- Action items and commitments
- Progress on previous commitments
- Focus areas for next session
