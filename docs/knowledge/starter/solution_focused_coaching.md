# Solution-Focused Coaching

What is the miracle question in coaching? The miracle question asks the client to imagine their problem has been solved overnight and describe what would be different — a core solution-focused technique for shifting from problem talk to desired futures.

Solution-focused coaching (SFC) emphasises what the client wants to achieve and what is already working, rather than analysing the history or root cause of problems. It is brief, future-oriented, and practical.

## Core assumptions

- Change is constant; small steps lead to larger shifts.
- The client already has resources and past successes to build on.
- Focusing on problems can reinforce them; focusing on solutions opens new possibilities.
- If something works, do more of it. If it does not work, try something different.

## Key techniques

### The miracle question
"Suppose tonight a miracle happened and the problem you came here with was solved. When you wake up tomorrow, what would be different? What would you notice first?"

### Scaling questions
"On a scale of 1–10, where are you now regarding [goal]? What makes it a [X] and not lower? What would one point higher look like?"

### Exception finding
"When is the problem least present or absent? What is different in those moments? How did you make that happen?"

### Best hopes
"What are your best hopes from this coaching?" — clarifies direction without dwelling on the problem story.

## Coaching questions

- What is already going better than you expected?
- What small sign would tell you things are improving?
- Who would notice first if things got better, and what would they see?
- What have you tried that helped even a little?
- What is the smallest step you could take this week?

## When to use solution-focused coaching

- Client is stuck in problem narrative and needs forward momentum.
- Limited session time — SFC can produce actionable insight quickly.
- Complement to strengths-based work — both highlight capability and progress.
- When detailed root-cause analysis is not needed or wanted.

## Distinction from other approaches

Unlike CBT-informed coaching, SFC does not focus on challenging specific thoughts. Unlike MI, it does not centre ambivalence — it centres desired futures and existing exceptions. It can be combined with GROW for action planning after solution imagery.
