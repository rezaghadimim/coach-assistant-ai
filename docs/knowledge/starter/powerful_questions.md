# Powerful Coaching Questions — Reference Catalog

Powerful questions are open-ended, thought-provoking, and focused on the client's experience and agency. They invite reflection rather than yes/no answers and avoid leading or advice-giving.

## Characteristics of powerful questions
- Begin with "what", "how", "who", or "when" (rarely "why", which can feel accusatory)
- Focus on the present or future, not the past
- Invite discovery and ownership
- Are short and clear — one question at a time
- Create productive discomfort without distress

## Goal clarification
- What do you really want?
- What does success look like for you — specifically?
- How will you know when you've achieved this?
- What would you need to see, feel, or have in order to feel satisfied?
- What is the most important thing you want to accomplish?
- If you could have it exactly as you want it, what would that be?

## Exploring reality
- What is actually happening right now?
- What have you already tried?
- What is working, even partially?
- What is getting in the way?
- What assumptions are you making about this situation?
- What are you not saying that might be important?
- What would someone else notice about this situation?

## Generating options and possibility
- What could you do about this?
- What else? (repeat)
- If you had unlimited resources and no fear of failure, what would you try?
- What has worked in a similar situation before?
- Who do you know who handles this well — what do they do?
- What is the smallest possible step you could take?
- What would a bolder version of you do?

## Building commitment and accountability
- Which of these options will you act on?
- What is the first step, and when will you take it?
- On a scale of 1–10, how committed are you to this?
- What might get in the way, and how will you handle it?
- What support do you need?
- How will you celebrate this progress?
- When shall we check in on this?

## Exploring emotions and energy
- How does this situation make you feel?
- What emotion is most present for you right now?
- What would it feel like if this were resolved?
- Where do you feel this in your body?
- What gives you energy in this situation?
- What is draining your energy?

## Challenging assumptions and limiting beliefs
- What are you assuming that might not be true?
- What would change if that assumption turned out to be wrong?
- Where did this belief come from?
- Is this belief helping or hindering you?
- What would you need to believe differently to take this step?

## Values and meaning
- What matters most to you about this?
- What are you standing for when you make this choice?
- How does this align with what you value most?
- What kind of person do you want to be in this situation?
- What would you regret if you did nothing?

## Solution-focused questions
- When is this problem least present?
- What is different in those moments?
- On a scale of 1–10, where are you now? What would a half-point improvement look like?
- Suppose a miracle happened overnight and this problem was solved — what would be different tomorrow morning?

## Questions to use sparingly (with care)
- Why did you do that? (can feel accusatory — rephrase as "What led you to that choice?")
- Have you thought about…? (leads client toward your idea — offer as an option instead)
- Shouldn't you…? (prescriptive — replace with "What feels right to you?")
