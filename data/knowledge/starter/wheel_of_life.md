# Wheel of Life Exercise

The Wheel of Life is a visual coaching exercise that helps clients assess balance across key life areas, identify priorities, and set goals for improvement. It is one of the most common intake and review tools in life coaching.

## How the exercise works

1. **Choose life areas**: Typical categories include career, finances, health, relationships, family, personal growth, fun/recreation, physical environment, and spirituality. The client can customise labels.
2. **Rate each area**: On a scale of 1–10, the client scores current satisfaction in each area (1 = very dissatisfied, 10 = fully satisfied).
3. **Draw the wheel**: Each area is a spoke on a circle. Connecting the scores creates a visual "wheel" — bumps show imbalance.
4. **Reflect**: Which areas pull the score down? Which matter most right now? Where would a small improvement have the biggest impact?
5. **Set goals**: Pick one or two areas for focused coaching work. Define specific actions and review dates.

## Coaching questions for the wheel of life

- Which area surprises you — higher or lower than you expected?
- If you could improve one area by two points in the next three months, which would make the biggest difference?
- What is already working in your highest-scored area that you could apply elsewhere?
- Which low-scored area are you avoiding, and why?
- How does this picture align with what you said you want from coaching?

## Variations

- **Work-life wheel**: Focus only on professional domains (role, team, growth, workload, purpose).
- **Values-aligned wheel**: Label spokes with core values instead of life domains.
- **Future wheel**: Rate where the client wants to be in six months, then compare to today.

## When to use the wheel of life

- First or intake sessions to map the client's whole context.
- Progress reviews every few months to track balance shifts.
- When a client feels "stuck" but cannot name the issue — the visual often clarifies.
- Goal-setting when the client has many competing priorities.

## Common pitfalls

- Trying to improve every area at once — focus on one or two spokes per coaching cycle.
- Treating low scores as failure — the wheel is a snapshot for conversation, not a grade.
- Skipping the "why" behind scores — always explore what drives each rating.
