# Active Listening in Coaching

Active listening is a core coaching skill in which the coach gives full attention to the client, reflects back what they hear, and helps the client feel understood before moving toward solutions. It is the foundation of trust and insight in coaching conversations.

## What active listening involves

- **Full presence**: Put aside distractions, internal commentary, and the urge to fix. Listen to understand, not to reply.
- **Reflecting content**: Paraphrase what the client said in your own words. "It sounds like the deadline pressure is affecting your confidence."
- **Reflecting emotion**: Name the feeling beneath the words. "That seems frustrating — and maybe a bit isolating too."
- **Summarising**: Periodically gather key themes. "So far I am hearing three things: the role change, the team dynamics, and your desire for more autonomy."
- **Silence**: Allow pauses. Clients often need space to think before they reach deeper insight.

## Active listening vs. passive hearing

Passive hearing waits for a turn to speak. Active listening:
- Notices what is said and what is omitted.
- Checks understanding: "Did I get that right?"
- Follows the client's lead rather than steering toward a pre-formed solution.
- Uses minimal encouragers: "Go on," "Tell me more," "What else?"

## Coaching questions that support listening

- What is most important about that for you?
- What else comes up when you sit with that?
- What are you noticing as you describe this?
- What would you want me to understand about how this feels?

## Common mistakes

- Interrupting with advice before the client has finished exploring.
- Asking a new question before reflecting what was just shared.
- Matching the client's story with your own experience.
- Listening for the problem to solve instead of listening for meaning.

## When to prioritise active listening

- Early in a session when the client needs to feel heard.
- When emotions are high or the client seems stuck.
- Before goal-setting — understanding must precede action.
- In motivational interviewing, as part of the OARS skills (Reflective listening).
