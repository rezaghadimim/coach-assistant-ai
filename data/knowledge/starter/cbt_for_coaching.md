# CBT-Informed Coaching — Application Guide

What coaching techniques help with negative thinking? CBT-informed coaching uses Socratic questioning, thought records, and cognitive reframing to identify and shift unhelpful thinking patterns such as catastrophising, all-or-nothing thinking, and mind-reading.

Cognitive Behavioural Coaching (CBC) adapts principles from Cognitive Behavioural Therapy for use in non-clinical coaching settings. It helps clients identify and shift unhelpful thinking patterns that limit performance, wellbeing, and goal achievement.

## Important distinction for coaches
Coaches use CBT-informed techniques to support professional performance, personal growth, and resilience — not to treat mental health disorders. Clinical CBT for depression, anxiety disorders, or trauma is the domain of licensed therapists. If a client's distress is severe or persistent, refer to a mental health professional.

## Core CBT Model (ABC)

- **A — Activating event**: the situation or trigger ("I got critical feedback from my manager").
- **B — Beliefs/Thoughts**: the client's interpretation ("This means I'm incompetent").
- **C — Consequences**: emotional and behavioural outcomes (shame, avoidance, withdrawal).

Coaching intervenes at B — helping the client examine and reframe their interpretation.

## Common Unhelpful Thinking Patterns

| Pattern | Example | Coaching reframe question |
|---------|---------|--------------------------|
| All-or-nothing | "If I'm not perfect, I'm a failure" | "What does partial success look like?" |
| Catastrophising | "If this goes wrong, everything is ruined" | "What's the most realistic outcome?" |
| Mind-reading | "They must think I'm stupid" | "What evidence do you have for that?" |
| Personalising | "It's my fault the project failed" | "What other factors were involved?" |
| Should statements | "I should always know the answer" | "Where did that rule come from?" |
| Overgeneralising | "I always mess up presentations" | "Has there been a time this went well?" |
| Discounting positives | "That went okay but it doesn't count" | "Why does it not count?" |
| Emotional reasoning | "I feel anxious, so something bad will happen" | "What would a friend say from the outside?" |

## Coaching Techniques

CBT-informed coaching techniques for negative thinking include Socratic questioning, cognitive reframing, thought records, and behavioural experiments. These methods help clients examine unhelpful thinking patterns and build more balanced perspectives.

### Socratic questioning
Use guided questions to help the client examine their own thinking:
- "What is the evidence for and against this belief?"
- "What would you say to a friend in this situation?"
- "Is there another way to interpret this?"
- "What is the impact of holding this belief?"

### Behavioural experiments
Collaborate on a small experiment to test a belief in real life:
- "You believe speaking up in meetings always leads to embarrassment. What small test could we design this week to check that belief?"

### Thought records
Ask the client to track situations, automatic thoughts, emotions, and alternative perspectives between sessions.

### Strengths and evidence log
Collect evidence that contradicts a negative self-belief to build a more balanced view over time.

## Coaching Questions for Cognitive Reframing

- What is going through your mind right now?
- What does this situation mean about you?
- How true is that thought, on a scale of 0–100?
- What would you need to see to believe it a little less?
- What is a more balanced way to look at this?
- If this thought were less powerful, what would you do differently?

## When to use CBC in coaching

- Client reports persistent self-doubt, imposter syndrome, or performance anxiety.
- Client is stuck in a pattern of avoidance or procrastination linked to fear of failure.
- Client sets goals but consistently undermines themselves.
- Unhelpful thought patterns appear to be the primary barrier to progress.
